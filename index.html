Italian present tense, for the trip

Start here

Most of the present tense is six endings

An Italian verb changes its ending to show who is doing the action. Learn the endings for the three verb families, then the handful of everyday verbs that break the rules. These ten patterns cover almost everything you'll say in a bar, a restaurant or a shop.

Pattern 1

Who is doing it, and why you can skip the pronoun

PronounMeansWhen you'll use it

Leiyou (polite)Waiters, shop staff, strangers. It uses the he/she form.
noiweOrdering for your group, plans
voiyou (plural)Asking a shop or bar: Avete…? = Do you (all) have…?
lorotheyOverheard talk about other people

The ending already tells you who is acting, so Italians usually drop the pronoun. Parlo means "I speak" on its own. Add io only for emphasis: Pago io! = "I'm paying!"

Travel tip

When a waiter says Cosa prende? they mean "What will you have?" The polite Lei borrows the he/she form, so prende can mean "he takes", "she takes" or "you (polite) take".

Pattern 2

The three families: -are, -ere, -ire

Chop off the last three letters of the infinitive to get the stem (parl-are, prend-ere, dorm-ire), then add the ending.

  • io always ends in -o, tu always in -i, and noi always in -iamo. That holds for almost every verb, irregular ones included.
  • voi keeps the infinitive's vowel: parlare → parlate, prendere → prendete, dormire → dormite.
  • loro is the he/she form plus -no: parla → parlano. For -ere and -ire the e becomes o: prende → prendono.
  • -ere and -ire are identical except in the voi form.

Pattern 3

The -isc- verbs

Some -ire verbs slip -isc- between the stem and the ending in every form except noi and voi. You can't tell from the infinitive, so memorize the common ones: capire (understand), preferire (prefer), finire (finish).

Say it: -isco sounds like "EES-ko" but -isci and -isce sound like "EE-shee" and "EE-sheh". Non capisco ("I don't understand") is one of the most useful sentences you can know.

Pattern 4

Spelling rules that keep the sound

  • -care / -gare add an h before i, so the hard "k" or "g" sound stays: pagare → paghi, paghiamo; cercare → cerchi, cerchiamo.
  • -iare verbs don't double the i: mangiare → mangi (not mangii), mangiamo. The same goes for cominciare, lasciare and consigliare.
  • -ere verbs don't add the h, so the sound shifts: conoscere → conosco ("ko") but conosci ("shee").

Pattern 5

Irregular verbs follow the "boot"

Put the singular forms on the left and the plurals on the right. In most irregular verbs the odd forms are io, tu, lui/lei and loro, which together make the shape of a boot. noi and voi usually stay regular.

irregular form (the boot)regular ending
  • The -go verbs: venire → vengo, vengono; rimanere → rimango, rimangono. If io ends in -go, loro ends in -gono.
  • io and loro often match: vado / vanno, faccio / fanno, voglio / vogliono, esco / escono.
  • Short verbs (dare, fare, stare, andare, sapere) use the same mini-endings: -ai, -a, -anno: dai/dà/danno, fai/fa/fanno, stai/sta/stanno, vai/va/vanno, sai/sa/sanno.

Pattern 6

The big four: essere, avere, andare, fare

These are fully irregular and show up in nearly every conversation. Learn them first.

Where English says "be", Italian often says "have" or "do"

Ho fame I'm hungry · Ho sete I'm thirsty · Ho fretta I'm in a hurry · Hai ragione You're right
Fa caldo / freddo It's hot / cold · Faccio colazione I have breakfast · Facciamo una passeggiata We take a walk

Pattern 7

volere, potere, dovere + an infinitive

"Want to", "can" and "must" are followed straight by the unchanged infinitive, just like in English. Conjugate only the first verb.

Posso pagare con la carta?Can I pay by card?
Devo prendere il treno.I have to catch the train.
Vogliamo vedere il Duomo.We want to see the cathedral.
The polite one: vorrei

Voglio ("I want") sounds blunt when ordering. Say vorrei ("I would like"), or vorremmo for a group. It isn't present tense, but you'll use it constantly: Vorrei un caffè, per favore. Just as natural: Prendo un caffè ("I'll have an espresso").

Pattern 8

piacere: "it pleases me"

Italian has no direct verb for "to like". Instead, the thing you like is the subject: "it is pleasing to me". So the verb agrees with the thing, not with you.

Mi piace il vino.I like the wine. (one thing)
Mi piacciono le olive.I like the olives. (more than one)
Mi piace camminare.I like walking. (an activity takes piace)
Ti piace? / Le piace?Do you like it? (friend / polite)
Ci piace Roma.We like Rome.

Pattern 9

One tense, three jobs

  • Right now: Bevo un caffè = I'm drinking an espresso.
  • Habits: Bevo un caffè ogni mattina = I drink an espresso every morning.
  • Plans and the near future: Domani vado a Firenze = Tomorrow I'm going to Florence. Add a time word (domani, stasera, venerdì) and the present tense covers your plans.

Pattern 10

Questions and "not"

  • Negative: put non right before the verb: Non mangio carne = I don't eat meat.
  • Question: keep the same words and raise your voice at the end: Prendete la carta? = Do you take cards?
  • Question words go first: Quanto costa? How much is it? · A che ora apre? What time does it open? · Dove vai? Where are you going?
